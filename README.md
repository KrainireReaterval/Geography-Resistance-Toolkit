# GEOG395 Toolkits

## Theme
**"A Toolkit for Resisting Algorithmic Empire: How Communities Can Fight Back Against Biometric and Predictive Surveillance"**

* This hits the assignment brief perfectly: it's anti-imperial, it's technology-specific, and it draws directly on your GEOG 258 work, your Beijing AR lab experience, and SkinGraph's dataset bias angle.
* It also mirrors the AI Now format naturally: a real problem, real issue areas, real interventions.

---

## HTML Website Structure

### 1. Hero Section Cover + Framing Statement
* **Contents:** Title, your name, one-sentence thesis.
* **Example Thesis:** *"Surveillance technologies are not neutral tools - they encode imperial logics of control, extraction, and racial classification. This toolkit maps how communities can refuse them."*

### 2. Opening The Problem: What Is an Algorithmic Empire?
* **Definition:** Define the concept. 
* **Sources:** Draw on Ruha Benjamin, Virginia Eubanks, and your GEOG 258 readings.
* **Context:** Connect facial recognition, predictive policing, and biometric databases to historical imperial practices of categorization and control.

### 3. Question — Who Gets Watched? (The Stakes)
* **Visuals:** A map or visual showing who surveillance technology disproportionately targets — by race, immigration status, geography.
* **Case Study:** This is where your Beijing AR lab experience can appear as a case study.

### 4. Issue Areas
* **Format:** Toggle list (once clicked, expands), mirroring AI Now's structure.
* **Each area contains:** What it is $\rightarrow$ Why it matters $\rightarrow$ What you can do
* **Key Areas:**
  * **Biometric Data Collection:** Refuse facial recognition at airports, stadiums, schools.
  * **Predictive Policing Algorithms:** Community demands for algorithmic audits.
  * **Dataset Bias & Training Data:** How biased datasets perpetuate harm (*SkinGraph* case).
  * **Cross-Border Data Flows:** Who owns your data when you live between states/nations.
  * **Institutional Adoption:** How to pressure universities, employers, landlords to reject surveillance tech.
